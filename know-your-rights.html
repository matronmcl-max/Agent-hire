Know your rights

Plain-words explainers for the situations people actually hit. Not legal advice — just what to know, and what to do first.

Benefits denied or cut off

What it is: an office says no, or stops your benefits, and sends a letter with a deadline.

  • Do not miss the deadline. Appeals usually have a short window — days, not months. The date is in the letter.
  • Ask for the reason in writing. You have a right to know why, in plain words.
  • Appeal, even if you are not sure. A "no" is often just the first answer, not the final one. Many decisions are changed on appeal.
  • Keep everything. The letter, the date you got it, every call, every name you spoke to.

A ticket or a fine

What it is: a ticket, a fine, or a court date you did not expect.

  • Read the whole ticket. The date, the court, and what you are accused of are all on it.
  • Do not ignore a court date. Missing it usually makes things worse — a warrant, a bigger fine, a suspended license.
  • You can usually ask for a hearing. That is your chance to explain your side, in your own words.
  • If you cannot pay, say so. Many courts will set up a payment plan or reduce a fine if you ask and explain.

A landlord notice

What it is: a notice to pay, to fix something, or to leave.

  • A notice is not an eviction. Only a court can order you out. A notice is the first step, not the last word.
  • Keep the notice, and note the date you got it. Deadlines in these letters matter.
  • Talk to the landlord in writing if you can. Text or email leaves a record. Keep copies.
  • Get help fast if it says "court" or gives you a short deadline. Legal aid and tenant help exist for exactly this — do not wait.

A bank or app flags your account

What it is: your account is frozen, a payment is held, or you are locked out — and the reason is vague.

  • Ask for the reason in writing. "System decision" is not a reason. Ask what rule, and what you can do about it.
  • Ask how to appeal. Banks, Cash App, and other apps all have a dispute or appeal path — it is often buried, but it exists.
  • Keep records. Screenshots, dates, reference numbers, and the names of anyone you spoke to.
  • Do not send money to "fix" it. No real bank or app will ask you to pay a fee to unfreeze your own account. That is a scam.

Stopped, searched, or locked up

What it is: police stop you, search you, or hold you — and you are not sure what your rights are.

  • You have the right to remain silent. You do not have to answer questions beyond your name. Say clearly: "I am exercising my right to remain silent."
  • You have the right to ask if you are free to leave. If you are not under arrest, you can ask to go.
  • You do not have to consent to a search. You can say: "I do not consent to a search."
  • If you are locked up, you have the right to challenge it. That is habeas corpus — the government has to show a judge why you are being held. Ask for a lawyer, and ask for a hearing.
  • Stay calm, remember names and badge numbers if you can, and write down what happened as soon as you can.
